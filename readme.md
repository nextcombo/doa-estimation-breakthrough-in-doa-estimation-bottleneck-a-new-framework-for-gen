2. Abstract This paper proposes a generalized framework for direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation based on subspaces in unknown noise fields and other non-ideal conditions. Based on the estimation of the noise covariance matrix, a forward-only and its forward-backward extension root value method DOA estimator is designed. Introduction Direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation has been an active research field for decades, thanks to its important applications in wireless communication, automotive radar, sonar, and many other traditional and emerging fields. At the same time, source localization holds significant importance in many practical scenarios.
The typical assumptions for observing noise's second-order statistical characteristics include uniform white noise (uniform scaling identity matrix), non-uniform white noise (non-uniform scaling identity matrix), and block-correlated noise (block diagonal covariance matrix). For the scenario of uniform white noise (i.e., all array sensors have the same noise power), various classical subspace methods such as MUSIC, root-MUSIC, ESPRIT have been developed. Compared to the computationally intensive maximum likelihood (ML) estimators, subspace methods offer good estimation accuracy with moderate computational complexity, making them more favored.
However, when the related assumptions (beyond the basic assumption of uniform noise, including large sample size, signal statistical independence, high signal-to-noise ratio, sufficient source space separation, and few sources) cannot be fully satisfied, there will be a performance gap between subspace methods and ML methods. In recent years, numerous studies have attempted to bridge this performance gap by developing methods that address challenges such as data sample scarcity, low signal-to-noise ratio, correlated or even coherent sources. Techniques like root-swap root-MUSIC, EPUMA, SE GLS, UE GLS aim to tackle these scenarios. Additionally, forward-backward averaging (FBA) and forward-backward spatial smoothing (FBSS) are often used to improve subspace methods for dealing with correlated or coherent signals.
Recent advancements in sparse Bayesian learning (SBL) have led to various outlier DOA estimation methods that achieve high precision without resorting to dense discreteization. However, existing methods still face limitations such as sensitivity to correlated sources, high computational complexity, poor performance in close sources, small sample sizes, low signal-to-noise ratio, and scenarios with many sources.
To address these shortcomings, this paper proposes a general framework for DOA estimation in uniform linear arrays (ULAs) under unknown sensor noise environments, which overcomes the limitations of existing methods. The framework first estimates the noise covariance matrix, followed by a two-stage process to achieve high-precision DOA estimation, providing a new effective solution for DOA estimation in complex real-world scenarios.
IV. Method Overview (I) Core Framework This broad framework is based on an Uniform Linear Array (ULA), designed for unknown noise field environments, with two main stages:
1  
DOA Candidate Estimation Phase: First, the problem of DOA estimation under unknown noise is transformed into a uniform noise scenario by pre-whitening processing. Then, based on the characteristics of signal subspace perturbation and Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT), a root value method DOA estimator based on Generalized Least Squares (GLS) is designed to estimate polynomial coefficients and obtain DOA candidates through root finding. To handle correlated sources, an extended version of this estimator in forward-backward (FB) direction is also derived. By running this estimator twice, DOA candidates are generated that are twice the actual number of sources (2L).
2  
DOA selection phase: A three-step selection strategy is proposed, which incorporates traditional beamforming (CB), maximum likelihood (ML) optimization, and Generalized Likelihood Ratio (GLR) techniques. The CB is used to eliminate outliers from the candidate set, the GLR is employed to select the optimal initial DOA, while ML optimization determines the final L DOA estimates from the remaining candidates, significantly reducing computational complexity in the selection process.
(II) Key technical details 1
Pre-whitening processing: Preprocess the received signal by taking the inverse square root of the noise covariance matrix, which converts non-uniform noise into uniform noise, laying a foundation for subsequent estimation.
2  
SVD Decomposition and Subspace Relation: Perform truncation singular value decomposition (SVD) on the preprocessed signal. Utilize the subspace of the signal and the column space of the array manifold matrix to establish a linear equation system.
3  
Alternating Iterative Optimization: Utilize an alternating strategy to estimate the weight matrix and polynomial coefficients in GLS, derive the weight matrix using a first-order approximation of signal subspace perturbation, and achieve high precision with only 3-5 iterations.
4  
Forward-backward expansion: By constructing a centered eigenmatrix, the decoupling of correlated sources is processed to further improve estimation accuracy.
(III) Computational Complexity
The computational complexity of this method primarily stems from the SVD decomposition, DFT transformation, matrix inversion, and DOA selection processes. By designing a reasonable selection strategy, its computational complexity is approximately one-fourth that of existing EPUMA methods, ensuring performance while also considering computational efficiency.
V. Conclusion
This paper proposes a general framework for DOA estimation applicable to non-ideal scenarios (including unknown noise fields, correlated sources, close-range sources, large number of sources, low SNR, small sample sizes, etc.). Based on the estimation of the noise covariance matrix, it combines GLS with first-order signal subspace perturbation design root value method to generate 2L DOA candidates. The final optimal estimate is selected through fusion strategies of CB, GLR, and deterministic ML. The forward-backward extension version further enhances the capability to handle correlated sources, and has been proven to be consistent. Numerical simulation results show that under various complex challenging scenarios, this general framework significantly outperforms existing advanced methods, providing an efficient and reliable solution for DOA estimation problems in practical engineering applications.
